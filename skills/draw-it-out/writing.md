# draw-it-out: writing

Every word the user reads goes through humanizer: answers, notes, quizzes, tours and prep. If the `humanizer` skill is installed (github.com/blader/humanizer), load it once and use it in embedded mode: run its checks silently and output only the final text. If it isn't installed, follow the rules below, condensed from it.

Leave the fixed parts exactly as they are and humanize only the prose inside them: headings, the bold labels in the notes layout, the fixed lines of the reply shapes in SKILL.md ("How it works:", "Proof:", "Checked on", "Remember this:", "Stop N of M", "What you see:", "What happens underneath:", "Score:"), status emoji and words, tables, proof paths, code and frontmatter.

## Rules

- Say it plainly. Use "is", "has" and "does", not "serves as", "stands as", "boasts" or "features".
- Cut AI vocabulary: delve, crucial, pivotal, vital, robust, seamless, leverage, streamline, utilize, comprehensive, cutting-edge, landscape, realm, tapestry, testament, underscore, foster, enhance, empower, elevate, navigate, journey, unlock.
- Don't pad with significance. A feature doesn't "play a key role"; say what it does.
- No sales voice: powerful, effortless, intuitive, game-changing, best-in-class. The status label carries the judgement.
- No "not just X, but Y", "it's not X, it's Y" or "no X, no Y, just Z".
- Don't group things in threes unless there are three things.
- No em dashes or en dashes. Use a full stop, comma, colon or brackets.
- No "-ing" tails that add fake analysis, like ", ensuring a smooth experience".
- No vague sources such as "experts say" or "it's widely considered". Name the file, or say "not verified".
- One hedge at most, and only when the doubt is real. Never "may potentially" or "could possibly".
- No filler openers or closers: "Great question", "In summary", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if".
- Sentence case in headings. Bold only for the fixed labels.
- Short sentences, active voice, everyday words. Let sentence length vary a little.
- Never add a fact to make text sound natural. Every claim still needs proof from the code.
